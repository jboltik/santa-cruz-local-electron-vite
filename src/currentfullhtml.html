1 /  EXCLUSIVE

They gave him a home. Then they took it away.

The Mental Health Client Action Network clubhouse has been shuttered since August. (Amaya Edwards — Santa Cruz Local/CatchLight Local)

For over 50 years, the nonprofit Mental Health Client Action Network was run by and for people with mental illness, and helped them with food, health care and social connection. In summer 2025, it abruptly shuttered amid financial difficulties. Nearly six months later it remains closed, and its future uncertain.

As a member of MHCAN’s night watch, Ari Hutchinson, 44, cleaned the clubhouse and attended to after-hours visitors, many with severe mental illness looking for help. He lived on site, and his savings slowly grew. He planned to eventually rent his own home.

Then, in July, the paychecks stopped coming. A month later, MHCAN’s executive director was fired by the organization’s board. And finally, in November, the organization’s board of directors told Hutchinson and three other employees living in the building to leave.

County Health Services Agency staff are working with the remaining leaders of the organization to untangle its finances and re-open the facility. But some former employees and members are left without answers, and without help.

“The first time, I pulled myself out (of homelessness) by sheer will,” Hutchinson said. “This time, it’s like I can’t even handle being cold.”

2 /  HOUSING

Meetings set for Pacific Ave. developments

A rendering shows a proposed eight-story building at 407 Pacific Ave. in Santa Cruz. (CRP Affordable Housing and Development)

Large developments proposed in the City of Santa Cruz are required to have public meetings for residents to ask questions and voice concerns. Two meetings are planned for housing proposals on Pacific Avenue this week:

  • 6 p.m. Monday, Jan. 26 on Zoom: The proposed redevelopment of the Neptune Apartments at 407 Pacific Ave. into 102 apartments. Read more.
  • 6 p.m. Thursday, Jan. 29 on Zoom: The proposed redevelopment of the Catalyst at 1009 Pacific Ave. into 64 condominiums. Read more.

Separately, city staff are reviewing a permit application for a proposed six-story, 225-unit apartment building at 530 Ocean St. The proposal includes 39 below-market-rate homes and 84 parking spaces. 3 /  SANTA CRUZ CITY COUNCIL

Santa Cruz council to consider coastal resilience

Waves strike West Cliff in January. (B. Sakura Cannestra — Santa Cruz Local)

2:30 p.m. Tuesday, Jan. 27 /  Online and at 809 Center St., Santa Cruz

At its Tuesday meeting, the Santa Cruz City Council is set to review a report on community engagement efforts city staff have completed as part of a Resilient Coast Santa Cruz 2.0 Project.

Across three meetings, more than 90 residents participated, voicing concerns about coastal erosion, loss of beach area and the threat to surf breaks. Each meeting focused on a different area: West Cliff, Main Beach/Cowell Beach and Seabright/East Cliff.

A technical brief will also be presented, that synthesizes prior coastal planning, studies and reports. The brief identifies several gaps in the city’s ability to undertake projects to prepare for sea-level rise, including a plan for how to pay for such projects.

Next, city staff will identify possible priority projects for the next 20-30 years. More opportunities for residents to voice their concerns and priorities are expected in early spring.

Also on the agenda for Tuesday’s meeting are:

  • A 5% merit-based raise for City Manager Matt Huffaker. His new salary would be $366,204, not including benefits or special pay.
  • A nearly $2 million change order for a suite of water supply resiliency projects that has overshot its timeline by two years.
—Nik Altenberg

To participate: Join on Zoom or call 669-900-9128, meeting ID 946 8440 1344. To comment ahead of the meeting, email citycouncil@santacruzca.gov by 5 p.m. Monday. The meeting will be broadcast on Comcast channel 25 and streamed on the city’s website.

 

4 /  RAIL TRAIL

Rail line takeover gets pushback

The Santa Cruz Branch Rail Line in Capitola. (Stephen Baxter — Santa Cruz Local file)

Days after the Santa Cruz County Regional Transportation Commission declared its intention to take control of operations on the Santa Cruz Branch Rail Line, the CEO of Minnesota-based Progressive Rail Inc. pushed back in a letter to the commission.

The Jan. 23 letter, shared with Santa Cruz Local, called the move “factually baseless, legally invalid, and critically at odds with federal railroad regulatory law and policy.”

Although the transportation commission has owned the rail corridor since 2012, St. Paul and Pacific Railroad Company, a subsidiary of Progressive Rail, is the “common carrier” responsible for coordinating freight operation. The commission now wants to take over as a common carrier for most of the line.

The move would empower the commission to build an “interim trail” atop the train tracks through Eastside Santa Cruz, Live Oak, Soquel and Aptos.

Progressive Rail CEO said the company is willing to bring the fight to the U.S. Surface Transportation Board, which regulates freight railways, and to bring a lawsuit.

5 /  BOARD OF SUPERVISORS

County could reorganize veterans services

Dave Ramos, left, and Robert ‘Hutch’ Collier have advocated for changes to the Santa Cruz County Veterans Services Office. (Amaya Edwards — Santa Cruz Local/CatchLight Local)

9 a.m. Tuesday, Jan. 27 /  Online and at 701 Ocean St., Room 020, Santa Cruz

Following calls for county leaders to overhaul veterans services, the Santa Cruz County Board of Supervisors on Tuesday is set to consider transferring the county’s Veterans Services Office from the Human Services Department to the County Executive Office. If approved, the transfer would be effective March 2026.

The Veterans Services Office helps veterans manage their benefits from the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, including health care, education and disability pay.

A group of local veterans has alleged that as many as half of the county’s 7,500 veterans are not receiving the full benefits they’re entitled to, and that the veterans services office is hindered by ineffective management. They said the office should be its own department.

At Tuesday’s meeting, the board is also set to consider:

  • A report on the condition of county roads. The county spends about $8.5 million annually in paving, patching and utility-related repairs. But to keep roads from deteriorating further, they’d need nearly $30 million a year, according to a report from county staff.
  • An update on the “Streamline Santa Cruz County” program to improve and hasten the building permitting process, particularly for survivors of the 2020 CZU Lightning Complex Fire.
  • An update on county projects to mitigate greenhouse gas emissions and adapt to climate change.
—Jesse Kathan

To participate: Join on Zoom or call 669-900-6833 , meeting ID 160 123 6676. To comment ahead of the meeting, email BoardOfSupervisors@santacruzcountyca.gov by 5 p.m. Monday. The meeting will be streamed on Facebook.

 

6 /  CAPITOLA

Learn more about Capitola politics

Discover Capitola offers residents a chance to get involved with local politics and learn more about issues affecting their neighborhoods. (Amaya Edwards — Santa Cruz Local/CatchLight Local)

Capitola is holding four workshops in February and March offering residents an opportunity to learn more about how the city operates.

The goal is to empower residents to be more civically engaged, by understanding how financial decisions are made during budget season and learning about upcoming projects.

There will be networking time for attendees wanting to get more involved in local politics. Topics to be covered include an introduction to the governing structure of Capitola, public safety, local business and community identity.

There is no cost to participate.

Interested participants are required to register online by Jan. 28. For more information and questions, contact the Assistant to the City Manager, Chloé Woodmansee.

—Alexandria Bordas

 

7 /  CULTURE

Screening of film by Jennifer Newsom

A screening and panel discussion on Jan. 28 features ‘The Mask You Live In’, a 2015 film that explores the challenges of young men faced with staying true to themselves while navigating the expectations of masculinity.

It was written and directed by Jennifer Siebel Newsom, the first partner of California, wife of Governor Gavin Newsom.

The film screening is a joint effort between Monarch Services, a domestic violence shelter operator and service provider, and the Santa Cruz County Office of Education, in partnership with the Watsonville Film Festival. CineLux Capitola Café & Lounge will be showing the film.

The panel discussion will include Ashanti Branch of the #MillionMaskMovement, who is featured in the film, and local leaders.

The event starts at 5 p.m. with a tabling networking hour and the film starts at 6 p.m. A panel discussion and Q&A will follow the film at 7:30 p.m. The event is free and open to the public, with Spanish subtitles and panel interpretation available in Spanish. The film is recommended for audiences no younger than 15 years old.

—Alexandria Bordas

8 /  YOUR THOUGHTS

Your thoughts on nursing staff shortages, MHCAN

Watsonville Hospital could close ICU amid staffing shortage. Annabelle Covington, a registered nurse, addresses the board of directors at the hospital on Jan. 14. (Fidel M. Soto — Noticias Watsonville)

Santa Cruz Local strives to understand Santa Cruz County in all its complexity. We turn to you as experts on your neighborhood, and we look for solutions to common problems.

Santa Cruz Local loves Santa Cruz County and strives to understand it in all of its complexity. We also want to get to know our readers.

Last week we asked you: Are you a health care worker in Santa Cruz County? Have you faced burnout or staff shortages, and if so, what do you think would help?

Some responses, edited for length and clarity:

“As a nurse with 40+ years in critical care and as a nurse paralegal, I have watched nurses burn out not because they ‘can’t handle it,’ but because the system created a deliberate staffing crisis that crushes moral integrity and puts profit over human lives. Since Ronald Reagan moved health care onto the stock exchange in 1980, patient safety has taken a back seat to stockholder returns. I have marched for safe staffing since 1996 because this crisis is intentional. Nurses are ready to care; hospitals refuse to staff them so CEOs can protect their paychecks.

Nurses burn out because they must choose between their license, their ethics, and their livelihood. Nurses are not the problem. We are the only reason the system hasn’t collapsed. If the public wants safe care, they must demand clinical nurses on boards, safe staffing ratios, transparency, accountability, protect whistleblowers.” —Michelle Wahl, Santa Cruz County

“Yes, I work in health care and have experienced both burnout and staff shortages. Employers should ensure that they are listening to the needs of their employees, and making adjustments to whatever parts of the workflow are causing the most concern from their teams. Additionally, it would be in best interest for staff to have the freedom to make their own decisions on how to best deliver care to patients. We want to be a part of the solution in the best possible way, so, having the permission to be ourselves, with our unique ideas, would probably be beneficial to both employees and the patients who are counting on them.” —Kylie Wyldbore, Santa Cruz

—Jay Leedy
